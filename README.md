## Why It Happens

The root of nearly every hiccup is that timetable 3 mac doesn't keep its own separate database — it reads and
writes Apple Calendar events. That's the feature, not a bug: it's why there's no new app to learn and why
your hours sit right beside your appointments. But it also means the app is only as reliable as your Calendar
configuration. If a calendar is local-only, it won't sync to your other devices; if it's hidden, entries seem
to vanish; if Calendar permission was never granted, the app simply can't read anything. None of these are
faults in the tracking itself — they're the plumbing underneath.

Billing mismatches follow the same logic. Since time billing here is calculated from the events you've
logged, a wrong total almost always means an event is missing, doubled, or filed under the wrong project
rather than a miscalculation. Fixing the events fixes the invoice.

## Prevention Tips

The best way to avoid all of it is a clean setup on day one. Create a dedicated calendar for billable work on
a syncing account — not a local-only calendar — so entries reach every device and never disappear. Grant
Calendar access the first time download timetable 3 for mac asks, so it can read and write without silent
failures. Get into the habit of saving each work session as a single event with an accurate start and end
time, filed under the right project calendar, and your totals stay honest automatically.

Finally, before you send anything, glance over the date range and hourly rate. A quick review catches the
occasional overlapping event or stray entry, and from then on timetable 3 for mac quietly tracks time and
totals your billing without you having to troubleshoot it again.
